# The Steep Club — Launch Campaign Plan

**Product:** The Steep Club, a monthly tea subscription box from Kettlebird Tea
**Audience:** ~2,000 people on the waitlist
**Timeline:** 4 weeks, Monday 2 November – Sunday 29 November 2026
**Channel:** Email only. No paid ads.
**Budget:** Small.

> Planning document only. Nothing here sends mail. Per `CLAUDE.md`, no real
> audience is contacted from this studio.

---

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** The Steep Club Opens

**Summary:** Over four weeks, invite the waitlist to become the first members of
The Steep Club, one segment at a time, and convert 300 of them into paying
subscribers.

**Primary objective:** 300 waitlist members start a paid subscription during the
first month.

**Secondary objectives:**

- Learn which of the three segments converts best, so month two can focus there.
- Collect the first real customer quotes and reviews (we currently have none).
- Build the habit of a monthly send before the first box ships.

**A note on the target:** 300 out of 2,000 is a **15% conversion rate**. Waitlist
to paid usually lands between 5% and 15%, so this goal sits at the top of the
realistic band. It is reachable, but only if the list is still warm and the offer
is clear in the first email. See Risks.

## 2. Target Audience

The waitlist is one list with three different reasons for being on it. They get
the same product and different arguments.

| Segment | Who they are | What they need to hear | Likely size |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tea-curious beginners** | Students who drink tea occasionally, mostly tea bags, and are unsure what to do with loose leaf | "No gear, no ceremony. The box tells you what to do." | unknown — confirm |
| **Gift buyers** | People who joined thinking of someone else, often a parent or a friend | "It arrives every month without you remembering to send it." | unknown — confirm |
| **Daily tea drinkers** | Already drink tea every day and are tired of the supermarket shelf | "One properly chosen tea a month, instead of eight average ones." | unknown — confirm |

**Shared context:** many are students learning to become forward deployed
engineers — short on money and time, and tired of being sold to. They are on the
waitlist because they chose to be. That is the strongest asset this campaign has,
and the plan leans on it rather than on pressure.

**Buying stage:** Consideration to decision. They know the product exists and have
already raised a hand. The job is to remove doubt, not to create awareness.

**Where they are:** Email is the only channel we are using, which is fine — they
gave us the address on purpose.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** One box, one tea, once a month. The choosing is already done.

That is the whole promise: fewer decisions, not more. It works for a beginner who
does not know where to start and for a daily drinker who is bored of deciding.

**Supporting messages:**

1. **For beginners** — "Everything you need is in the box, including the
   instructions. Three minutes, water just off the boil, done."
2. **For daily drinkers** — "One tea, chosen properly, instead of a cupboard of
   ones you settled for."
3. **For gift buyers** — "It arrives every month on its own. You do not have to
   remember anything."
4. **For all three** — "You are on the waitlist, so you get in first. After that
   it opens to everyone."

**Proof points we actually have:** the tea itself and how to brew it, the fact
that this is a small shop, and the waitlist relationship.

**Proof points we do not have yet:** no customer reviews, no subscriber counts, no
press. Do not invent them. `brand/voice.md` §4 forbids made-up social proof, and
this campaign has to work without it.

## 4. Channel Strategy

**Email only.** One channel, done properly.

- **Why it fits:** the audience is a waitlist. They opted in. There is no cheaper
  or more direct route, and no budget for ads.
- **Format:** short plain-text-feeling emails, one idea each, one link. Light HTML
  at most.
- **Effort:** low to medium. The work is in the writing and the segmentation, not
  the build.
- **Budget:** mostly the email platform and a little for the landing page. There
  is no media spend to allocate.

**What we are deliberately not doing:** no paid social, no influencer seeding, no
PR push. With a small budget and four weeks, splitting attention would weaken the
one channel that is already warm.

**Segmentation plan:** send to all three segments with different opening
paragraphs and subject lines, not three separate campaigns. One list, three doors.

## 7. Success Metrics

| Metric | Target | How tracked |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid subscriptions** | **300** | Checkout, by segment tag |
| Landing page conversion | 25% of clicks | Web analytics |
| Open rate | 45%+ | ESP (a warm waitlist should beat a cold list) |
| Click rate | 4%+ | ESP |
| Unsubscribe rate | Under 1% per send | ESP |
| Gift subscriptions | 40+ | Checkout, gift flag |
| Segment winner identified | By 22 Nov | Conversion by tag |

**Reporting:** a short note after each week — sends, opens, clicks, subscriptions,
and one sentence on what to change. No dashboards.

**The number that matters:** 300. Everything else explains why it did or did not
happen.

## 8. Budget Allocation

Email only, so there is no media budget to split.

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Email platform | Existing cost, sized for a 2,000 list |
| Landing page | Time, or a small one-off if built by someone else |
| Photography of the box | One afternoon, phone camera is fine |
| Paid media | **Zero** |

The real cost is writing time: seven emails plus variants. Budget it as the main
expense.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **15% is at the top of the realistic range** | Missing the target looks like failure even if the campaign worked | Agree a floor now: 200 subscriptions is a good month. Report against both |
| **The waitlist has gone cold** | Signups may be months old, and a cold send to 2,000 hurts deliverability | Warm-up send to the most engaged 20% first; drop anyone who has not opened in 90 days |
| **No social proof** | Every launch email wants a testimonial and we have none | Lead with specificity instead: what is in the box, what it tastes like, what it costs. Never invent quotes |
| **One channel, no fallback** | If email underperforms there is no second lever | Front-load the offer in Week 1, and use Week 4 for the narrowed last call rather than another broad send |
| **Founding offer may not be real** | A fake cap would break §3 and §4 of the voice guide | Confirm the cap exists before Email 6. If not, cut the email |

## 10. Next Steps

1. **Confirm four facts before writing anything:** the price, whether
   cancellation is genuinely open, whether a founding-member cap is real, and
   what is in the first box.
2. **Get segment counts** from the waitlist so the calendar can be weighted.
3. **Build the landing page** — it gates the whole campaign.
4. **Write Email 1 and its three subject lines.** Nothing else can start until
   the launch email is right.
5. **Decide the reporting rhythm** — one short note per week, to whom.

- "One tea, chosen properly"
- "Better than the shelf you have been settling for"
- "This month's tea, and why it is this one"
